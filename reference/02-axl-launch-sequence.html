The AXL v1.0
Launch Sequence

A 24-week phased plan from foundation to ratification, structured against the precedent of CDP, OpenAPI, MCP, and adjacent specification launches.

Owner
AXO founding team, in coordination with axlspec.org editorial
Horizon
24 weeks (Phase 1 → Phase 5 final publication)
Status
Draft plan – to be ratified by founders before Phase 1 spend
Reviewed against
The AXL Reference v1.0 (companion document)

The five phases

Summary

  1. Phase 1Weeks −8 to 0Foundation – IP, infra, reference implementation, analyst pre-briefs
  2. Phase 2Weeks 0 to 1Public draft launch – manifesto and 60-day comment period
  3. Phase 3Weeks 1 to 8Recruitment – vendor outreach, working groups, technical writing
  4. Phase 4Weeks 8 to 16Comment integration – release candidates, first attestations, buyer's-guide push
  5. Phase 5Weeks 16 to 24Ratification – v1.0 final, certification opens, first analyst report cycle

The sequence is calibrated against three things at once: the timing of analyst-firm publication cycles (Forrester Wave nominations, Gartner Cool Vendor research windows), the need for a real public-comment period to maintain credibility as an open specification rather than a vendor artifact, and the operational reality of a small founding team. Compression below 18 weeks is possible but degrades credibility; expansion beyond 30 weeks invites a competitor to publish first.

Phase 1 · Foundation Weeks −8 to 0

Build the substrate before the world sees it.

Everything in this phase is invisible to the market. The goal is to make the public draft launch in Phase 2 look like the publication of a serious specification, not a marketing event.

Deliverables

  • F1Trademark filings. AXL wordmark (Class 9 + 42, 1(b) ITU) filed via counsel. Certification-mark filing initiated. Trademark policy drafted. See companion brief.
  • F2Domain and handle acquisition. axlspec.org, axl.dev, getaxl.com, theaxl.com registered. Social handles secured: GitHub axl-spec, X/LinkedIn @axlspec.
  • F3axlspec.org legal entity. Initial structure (likely AXO-controlled LLC) stood up. Operating agreement and editorial-board charter drafted. Counsel-recommended path to multi-stakeholder governance documented.
  • F4Reference implementation MVP. 500–1,000 LOC TypeScript, minimally L1-conformant, published privately at github.com/axl-spec/axl-reference. Repo public-ready by end of phase.
  • F5Specification site. axlspec.org built – single-page render of v1.0 spec, GitHub-backed for public comment, accessible URL structure (/spec/v1.0, /glossary, /conformance).
  • F6Analyst pre-briefings under embargo. 5–8 briefings booked: Forrester (Joe Stanhope or successor on martech), Gartner (martech VP analysts), IDC, Constellation, RedMonk. Each brief includes the v1.0 draft, the four-pillar argument, and the §10 landscape.
  • F7Press list and embargoed pitch. TechCrunch, MarTech Series, AdExchanger, The Information (martech beat), Protocol-equivalent. Pitch deck and Q&A backgrounder prepared.
  • F8Founding-sponsor disclosure. Decision on whether to recruit one or two co-sponsoring vendors before public draft (a smaller experimentation tool willing to co-sign – even an early-stage vendor is fine; the optics matter).

Owner and budget

Primary owner
Founder + outside trademark counsel + technical lead
Indicative spend
$25k–$45k (TM + legal + domains + reference impl. labor)

Exit criteria – Phase 1 → Phase 2

  • Trademark filings submitted (not yet registered).
  • axlspec.org live, the spec rendered, GitHub repos public-ready.
  • Reference implementation MVP runnable on a demo page.
  • Minimum 3 analyst pre-briefings completed under embargo.
  • Founder-team go/no-go review on the public-launch press kit.
Risk: the reference implementation is the easiest deliverable to underestimate. A non-runnable repo at launch undermines the spec's credibility more than any other single artifact. Resource accordingly.
Phase 2 · Public draft launch Weeks 0 to 1

Publish the manifesto. Open the comment window.

A single high-orchestration week. The spec, the press, the analyst quotes, and the public-comment period all go live within five business days.

Deliverables

  • L1v1.0 Draft published at axlspec.org/spec/v1.0. Mirror at axoapp.ai/axl. GitHub repo open for issues and ASP submissions.
  • L2Founder essay / launch post. Long-form, technical, on a publication that signals seriousness – Substack-class, not Medium. The essay frames the four pillars and the failure modes of the existing stack. Not pitchy.
  • L3Press coordination. 3–5 published pieces in the launch week. At least one technical-publication piece (The Information / Protocol / AdExchanger) and one analyst voice quoted in the press.
  • L4Hacker News / Lobsters / r/martech post. Calibrated, non-spammy, by a credible technical contributor (not an obvious marketing account).
  • L560-day public comment period opens. ASP submission process documented. Editorial board commits to weekly triage of issues during the comment period.
  • L6AXO × AXL lockup deployed across axoapp.ai, sales materials, product UI. The "AXO is the Agentic Experience Layer" line begins appearing in the wild.

Owner and budget

Primary owner
Founder (essay) + comms lead + technical lead (issue triage)
Indicative spend
$10k–$20k (PR support, design, launch logistics)

Success metrics – first 7 days

  • 5,000+ unique visitors to axlspec.org/spec/v1.0.
  • ≥3 published press / analyst pieces.
  • ≥10 GitHub issues / ASPs filed against the spec by external contributors.
  • ≥1 named §10.2 vendor publicly engaging (positively or critically; engagement is the metric).
  • ≥50 stars on github.com/axl-spec/axl-reference.
Risk: a quiet launch is much worse than a delayed launch. If the press coordination and analyst voices aren't lined up by the end of Phase 1, slip Phase 2 by a week rather than ship into silence.
Phase 3 · Recruitment Weeks 1 to 8

Drag the named vendors onto our turf.

A category with one vendor doesn't get a Magic Quadrant. The §10.2 vendors are named in the spec on purpose – this phase converts naming into engagement.

Deliverables

  • R1Direct outreach to the six §10.2 vendors (Mutiny, Dynamic Yield, Optimizely, Adobe Target, Klaviyo, Braze). Personal letters from the founder to each named vendor's product/strategy lead. Companion artifact: AXL Specification Contributor Invitation.
  • R2Working-group formation. Five working groups stood up – one per pillar plus one for governance. Open invitations on GitHub. Initial meetings scheduled (biweekly).
  • R3Pillar deep-dive technical writing. Four long-form posts, one per pillar, by AXO engineers, published to the AXL blog. Each one ends with an explicit ASP-call for open questions in that pillar.
  • R4Conference and podcast circuit. Speaking submissions to MarTech Conference, Lifecycle Marketing Summit, RevOps Co-Op, MEASURE Summit. Podcast bookings: Marketing Powerups, MarTech Tomorrow, B2B Growth, etc.
  • R5First self-attestations targeted. Soft outreach to 2–3 sympathetic vendors (smaller experimentation tools, customer-experience platforms with the right architecture) inviting them to file an L1 or L2 Conformance Statement. The first non-AXO attestation is an inflection point.
  • R6Maturity-model launch. §9 of the Reference extracted into a standalone landing page at axlspec.org/maturity – buyer-facing, self-locating, suitable for procurement pre-reads.

Owner and budget

Primary owner
Founder (vendor outreach) + technical lead (working groups) + content lead (writing/speaking)
Indicative spend
$30k–$60k (content production, conference travel, working-group ops)

Success metrics – by end of Phase 3 (week 8)

  • ≥2 of the six named vendors engaged in working-group discussions (public or private).
  • ≥1 non-AXO Conformance Statement published.
  • ≥4 pillar deep-dive posts published.
  • ≥3 conference speaking acceptances landed.
  • ≥30 ASPs filed against the spec.
Risk: if the named vendors completely ignore the spec and no third-party attestations emerge, the category-of-one optic hardens. Mitigation: have 1–2 sympathetic small-vendor attestations pre-arranged before Phase 3 starts.
Phase 4 · Comment integration Weeks 8 to 16

Process the comments. Earn ratifiability.

The comment period closes at week 8. The next 8 weeks are spent demonstrably integrating community input. Visible iteration is what separates a real specification from a marketing document.

Deliverables

  • C1Comment triage and disposition. Every ASP and issue receives a public response and a disposition (accepted, deferred, declined with rationale). The disposition log is itself a published artifact.
  • C2v1.0 Release Candidate 1 (RC1). Published at week 10. Tracks visible changes from the v1.0 Draft. ASP credits in the editorial section.
  • C3v1.0 Release Candidate 2 (RC2). Published at week 13. Final substantive revisions. Editorial-only changes thereafter.
  • C4Buyer's guide standalone publication. §11 of the Reference extracted as a 4–6 page PDF. Designed, branded, distributed to procurement-channel newsletters. This is the single most-forwarded artifact the spec produces.
  • C5TCO calculator. Interactive page at axlspec.org/tco, parameterized by the buyer's current stack. Generates a printable comparison.
  • C6Conformance test suite v0.1. Published at github.com/axl-spec/conformance-tests. Covers Appendix A.1–A.5 with executable artifacts. Required for verified certification (Phase 5).
  • C7Round 2 analyst briefings. Re-engage the Phase 1 analysts with the post-comment-period evolution of the spec, attestation activity, and named-vendor engagement.

Owner and budget

Primary owner
Editorial board (triage) + technical lead (test suite) + design (buyer's guide)
Indicative spend
$20k–$40k (test-suite engineering, design, second analyst-relations cycle)

Success metrics – end of Phase 4 (week 16)

  • 100% of submitted ASPs have a public disposition.
  • ≥2 substantive specification changes incorporated from external contributors.
  • RC2 published with no breaking changes pending.
  • Buyer's guide downloaded ≥1,000 times in its first 30 days.
  • Conformance test suite runnable end-to-end on the reference implementation.
Phase 5 · Ratification Weeks 16 to 24

Publish v1.0 final. Open certification. Earn the first report.

The point at which the spec stops being a AXO project and starts being industry infrastructure. Whether the analyst-coverage flywheel turns over depends on the previous four phases having actually delivered.

Deliverables

  • F1v1.0 Final published at week 16. Frozen specification text. Versioning policy (§12.2) takes over from this point – minor versions for clarifications, major version for breaking changes.
  • F2Verified certification opens. First applications accepted at axlspec.org/certification. AXO applies for L3. Sympathetic vendors apply for L1 or L2. Certification fee schedule and reviewer panel published.
  • F3v1.0 launch event. Half-day virtual conference. Founder keynote, working-group reports, panel of certified vendors, analyst Q&A. Recorded; clips become content for months.
  • F4Analyst-report cultivation. Coordinated push for inclusion in: Forrester Wave (next personalization-platform cycle), Gartner Cool Vendors (martech), IDC MarketScape. The named-vendor activity from Phase 3 is the predicate for these reports getting written.
  • F5v1.1 working-group kickoff. The minor-version cadence begins. Scope: refinements, additional Recommended capabilities, testing-methodology improvements. Public roadmap.
  • F6Multi-stakeholder governance roadmap. Public commitment to a path toward 501(c)(6) or Linux-Foundation-equivalent governance. Trigger conditions stated (e.g. ≥10 certified vendors → governance transition).

Owner and budget

Primary owner
Founder + editorial board + axlspec.org operations lead (new hire, possibly fractional)
Indicative spend
$40k–$80k (event production, certification process ops, sustained AR)

Success metrics – end of Phase 5 (week 24)

  • v1.0 final published with at least 5 named contributors beyond AXO.
  • ≥3 verified certifications issued (AXO + ≥2 others).
  • ≥1 first-tier analyst (Forrester / Gartner / IDC) committed to a category-naming report on a defined publication date.
  • "AXL" appearing in third-party RFPs at ≥5 enterprise buyers, observable through inbound mentions.
  • axlspec.org operating with a published governance roadmap and timeline to multi-stakeholder transition.
Risk: a v1.0 final that ships with no certifications, no analyst commitments, and no §10.2-vendor engagement is structurally worse than a delayed v1.0. If at week 14 the metrics for Phase 4 aren't trending toward Phase 5 readiness, slip the v1.0 final by 4–6 weeks rather than ratify on a hollow base.
