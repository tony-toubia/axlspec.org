Re: Contributing to The AXL Reference v1.0

You're named in the spec. Here's what that means, and what we'd like to invite you to do about it.

I'm writing as the founder of AXO and the founding sponsor of axlspec.org, the stewarding organization for The AXL Reference – a published technical specification that defines the Agentic Experience Layer as a category of personalization infrastructure. You can read the v1.0 draft at axlspec.org/spec/v1.0; it's open under CC BY 4.0, with a 60-day public-comment period now in motion.

Your platform is named in §10.2 of the specification, alongside five others. The framing is structural, not adversarial: we describe which of the four pillars – Perceive, Decide, Render, Learn – your product satisfies, and which would need to evolve to claim full conformance. We worked hard to be accurate. If we got something wrong, the disposition log at github.com/axl-spec/spec is where you correct it, on the public record, and that correction becomes part of v1.0 final.

I want to be direct about why we named you, and why we're writing.

A category needs more than one vendor to become real. The CDP definition calcified because Segment, mParticle, Tealium, and Bloomreach all argued about what counted; CDP Institute became citable because it adjudicated those arguments. The same dynamic is unavoidable here. The Agentic Experience Layer becomes a recognized category – written about by Forrester and Gartner, embedded in procurement RFPs, used as a buyer's frame – only if multiple credible vendors are publicly engaging with the specification. Otherwise it remains, accurately, a AXO marketing artifact.

We'd rather it be the former. So would, we suspect, you.

Three concrete invitations:

One – join the working group corresponding to your strongest pillar. There are five (Perceive, Decide, Render, Learn, Governance), each meeting biweekly on the public record. Working-group seats are open; contributing to v1.x earns you editorial-section credit.

Two – file ASPs (AXL Specification Proposals) where you disagree with the spec's current shape. The §5 pillar definitions are the most consequential and the most likely to need revision based on production experience your team has and we don't.

Three – when v1.0 final ships in roughly twenty weeks, consider publishing a Conformance Statement. You may attest at L1 (Functional), L2 (Production), or you may publish a partial statement that enumerates which pillars you satisfy and which are on roadmap. Either is a meaningful contribution to the category.

The AXL™ certification mark is administered by axlspec.org and governed under the policy at axlspec.org/trademark. Use of the mark requires verified certification – a process that opens at v1.0 final. AXO will be applying alongside any other vendor who wishes to. The certification process is symmetric; the mark is not a AXO asset.

I'll be candid: we benefit if this category becomes real. So does anyone else operating in it. The five-tool personalization stack – CDP plus segmentation plus A/B plus journey plus attribution – is structurally exhausted. Buyers feel it; analysts are starting to write about it. Whether the architecture that replaces it is named precisely, or named badly, or named by an analyst from outside our industry six quarters from now – that's the open question. We've taken a position. We'd like to know yours.

I'm at tony@axoapp.ai directly. Forty-five-minute working call, no slides, no sales conversation – would welcome it on whatever timeline works for you.
